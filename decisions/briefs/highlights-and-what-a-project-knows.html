Decision brief · 13 November 2026 · answered by Miky in the brainstorm session that day and in his comments on the Leaf page “Highlights and the intelligence layer”. This page writes his answers up for four decision records; his merge of their pull request accepts them.

What a project knows

How should a project keep what it learns, what it decides and what worries it, so that people and every app’s intelligence share one memory instead of each keeping its own?

Today

Every project learns, decides and worries the whole time it lives. Today that knowledge sits in conversations, documents and people’s heads, and every agent keeps its own private copy. We already keep it by hand, in four places no app reads as one:

  • Insights, by hand: Claude’s memory is a folder on Miky’s disk, one note per lesson with its “Why” line. Katerina’s Claude sees none of it.
  • A journal, by hand: our decision records and the Decided lines on issues.
  • Risks, by hand: Warning callouts and each project’s Later list.
  • Ship’s feedback loop, by hand: the sweep, which gathers every bug reported in Peek since the last update and checks the projects touched.

Peek has five highlight types in its code (Insight, Concern, Conclusion, Question, Summary), and no screen offers them. On 28 October Miky decided that no app offers highlights until their shape is decided “together with what reads them”. This is what reads them.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

  • It answers “how can intelligence be better than in today’s SaaS apps?”: the agent’s memory is the team’s own Insights, Journal and Risks. People can see it and edit it, every app shares it, and it lives on the company’s relay, not inside one vendor’s AI.
  • It sets three shared types that outside apps build on, the way they build on an Issue. An outside company’s tracker, design tool or agent writes and reads the same Insight, Journal entry and Risk.
  • It is the input the Universal agent project waits for.

One-way door. Once items are on the relay and other apps read them, what an Insight, a Journal entry or a Risk means can change only compatibly; a breaking change is a new type. Who may mark and confirm, and how intelligence reaches a person, are two-way in the code but set every app’s expectations.

1 · What an item is

A · A mark on the source only

A highlight is a flag on the message or block, as upstream Nostr highlights work. Miky and Katerina see a coloured flag on her message, and nothing in the project. An outside company gets a flag it can show. What could go wrong: nothing can be questioned, refined or closed; a decision has no “decided by”; one insight from three messages is three flags.

B · Do less: text in the project’s description

The Overview keeps sections for what we learned, decided and worry about, as our Progress and Decided lines do today. What could go wrong: no app or agent can list, filter or confirm one entry; each edit rewrites the whole description; what is said in Peek or Leaf never reaches it.

C · Each item is its own file of a shared type Miky’s answer

Three types Estiva curates in public, like Issue: Insight (what we learned), Journal entry (what we decided or what happened: kind Decision or Update, with “decided by” kept apart from who wrote it) and Risk (what needs our oversight: kind Concern or Question). Each has a title, a summary, details, its sources, its home, a status and a conversation. Companies add properties and statuses on top. Marking a Peek message or a Leaf block makes one; in Leaf any block can be marked, tables included, and draws with a border. Peek and Leaf suggest one as you write. Peek’s highlight types fold in: Insight → Insight; Conclusion → Journal entry; Concern, Question → Risk; Summary is dropped, because every file has its own summary. What could go wrong: three new shared types are hard to change once outside apps use them.

D · Template databases each company reshapes

Estiva ships the three as starting databases a company copies and changes freely. What could go wrong: two companies’ Risks no longer mean the same thing, so an outside app or agent cannot rely on them; and people’s databases come only with the Notion-like app.

The statuses: a shared start, each type’s own end

Turned down: one status set for all three (Proposed, Accepted, Rejected, Miky’s first draft), because an Insight goes out of date, a decision is replaced and a risk is resolved, and one set cannot tell those apart; and two status fields, one for triage and one for the item’s life, because they drift apart.

2 · Where an item lives

  • Miky’s answer: as specific as possible: a task, else the project, else the Initiative. Choosing the home is secondary: an app or agent suggests it, and “Mark as highlight” may name a task, a project or an Initiative.
  • Turned down: several homes per item. The roll-up already shows a task’s items on its project, so a second home only shows them twice, and every app would need rules for whose readers see it.
  • What could go wrong: an item on a task some people cannot read stays hidden from them on the project too; a moved item’s conversation stays where it was first filed, as a moved issue’s does.

3 · Who marks and who confirms

  • Miky’s answer: anyone who can read a message or a block can mark it or unmark it, other people’s included. “Katerina is shy to mark it, I will do it for her.” Anyone can confirm a Proposed item, a “flexible system”: triage is the team’s job, so once Katerina confirms one, it is gone from Miky’s triage too.
  • Added by Claude, not objected to: an agent never confirms its own suggestion. What an agent proposes waits for a person.
  • Turned down: only the author marks (Katerina’s insight would stay unmarked); only the person who decided confirms a Journal entry (it stalls when they are away, and the entry already says who decided).
  • What could go wrong: a mark on someone else’s words is visible to the team; the item’s history says who made it. Unreviewed suggestions pile up unless the tab keeps them readable.

4 · How intelligence reaches you

  • Miky’s answer: “Intelligence is working for you async and it doesn’t try to steal your attention.” What an app or agent finds lands in its home, and you look when you go there. Only a message that mentions you reaches your Desk, exactly as a person’s does. Ship Home’s list of projects shows which have something new, with the Activity indicator’s rules: blue for something to read, orange for something to know, no counts.
  • Turned down: a separate intelligence inbox or notification stream. It competes with people for attention and grows with every app and agent added.
  • What could go wrong: a Proposed item nobody looks at stays unseen; how a tab or a stale Risk invites review without notifying anyone is shaped with the build.

How others do it

  • Upstream Nostr highlights are a mark pointing at the text it quotes: option A. Fine for reading notes; nothing to confirm or close.
  • Notion gives teams decision logs and risk registers as template databases each team reshapes: option D. Their meaning differs per workspace, which is fine inside one vendor and breaks across apps on an open protocol.
  • Linear keeps decisions and updates as project updates inside its own app; no other app can read or add one.
  • Today’s SaaS AI keeps its memory private to its vendor and sends digests and notifications of its own: the attention model Miky turned down.

The Ship project, for context (not a record)

Miky’s lean for the build: a Ship project has five tabs, Overview · Issues · Insights · Journal · Risks. The tab names are a screen call that goes on the build issue as a Decided line, not a record.

Why Journal and not Log: in software, “log” means machine output nobody reads (Miky, 13 Nov). A Journal entry’s kind is Decision or Update.

Recommendation

Take Miky’s answers as the four records: C, each item its own file of a shared type, with a shared start and each type’s own end; one home, rolled up; anyone marks and confirms, and no agent confirms itself; intelligence lands in its home and reaches a person only by naming them. The reason is the protocol and the person: a shared type is what lets an outside company’s app and agent read and add to the same memory, and a quiet, home-first model keeps people’s attention for people.

Checked

  • Peek’s code holds five highlight types, Insight, Concern, Conclusion, Question and Summary, and no screen offers them (Peek on main, 13 Nov). There is no “Update” type in Peek; Update is a kind of Journal entry.
  • The Accepted rule on resolving threads (28 Oct) offers no highlights until their shape is decided “together with what reads them”. These records answer that; they do not replace it.
  • The Accepted rules on types (26 and 30 Oct): one schema shape in an app’s listing or a person’s database, app types first; Estiva’s public directory of shared types; every new type starts with one named record; each company names its own statuses under shared stages.
  • Today every type shares one set of four stages (open, started, done, dropped); these three types bring their own, so that set becomes per type.
  • Accepted rules already cover a file’s summary as its own field, Leaf writing any file’s shared text, one conversation in every app, a file made under another being read by its readers, and an agent’s profile naming its owner.
  • Not checked: how Notion, Linear and others name these features today; the lines above are what each model does, not product claims.

What your merge accepts that you did not say in these words

  • An agent never confirms its own suggestion. Claude added it on the Leaf page; you did not object.
  • The fields beyond title, summary, details and conversation: sources, home, status, a Journal entry’s kind and “decided by”, and a Risk’s kind (Concern or Question), from the page’s table, which you commented on without changing.
  • “Initiative” stands for whatever today’s topics end up being called.

Parked and still open

  • Loops, intelligence an app brings that runs on its own: not in the first version, so no record (Miky). When one may mention someone, and how often, waits with them.
  • What intelligence costs, and who pays: much later.
  • A highlight from a private DM into a project the team reads: brainstormed again with private teams and notes.
  • The name Initiatives, waiting on Katerina’s look at the navigation prototype.
  • Whether a person’s own mark starts at Proposed or is already confirmed, and where an item lives that fits no task, project or Initiative: shaped with the build.
  • Order of work: highlights come after the Activity indicator, as Miky set on 10 November.

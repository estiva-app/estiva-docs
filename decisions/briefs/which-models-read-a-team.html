Which models read us

Open call 1 of "The intelligence layer: where we are going", after your answers of 19 Nov. About five minutes.

When a company picks the AI models its apps and agents use, can one team say "not that model for our conversations"?

Today

Two rules disagree. Peek's own rule says a work thread never goes to a cloud model, so its ⌘K answers only on your device. Claude, through the Estiva plugin, reads every team its agent is in and sends it to Anthropic. Nobody decided between them; they were set one app at a time.

On 19 Nov you settled most of the question. Each company hosts everything itself, and models are model-agnostic, as in Buzz: the provider is a setting (Anthropic, the company's own model, any OpenAI-compatible endpoint). And it's already decided that an agent reads only the teams it's in, and that its owner can cut it off at once. So the company chooses its providers. What's left is whether that choice is one switch for the whole company, or whether a team can narrow it.

Today. Peek keeps everything on the device; the Claude plugin sends whatever its agent can read.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

It sets the rule every app and every agent follows before it hands a team's words to a model, including an outside company's app. The rule itself is a two-way door: a setting can be added or loosened later. The words aren't: once a conversation reaches an outside provider, it can't be taken back. And a narrowing added later only binds apps built after it. An app written meanwhile won't check it.

Options

A. One switch for the company (do less)

An admin picks the models once. Every app and agent may send any conversation it can read to any of them. Peek's on-device-only rule is retired on purpose.

You and Katerina: one choice when Estiva is set up; afterwards every app answers with the same strong model, Peek's ⌘K included.

An outside company: it has to choose for its most sensitive team. A fintech either keeps HR and disputes off Claude by giving it up for everyone, or accepts that HR goes out too.

What could go wrong: a private team (HR, a deal room) gets created after the switch and quietly inherits a frontier cloud model nobody chose for it.

B. The company sets models, and a team can narrow them

The admin's list is the most any team gets. A team can say "only the device and our own model". Every app and agent reads the team's setting before sending its words to a model, as each already checks who may read the team.

You and Katerina: nothing changes for your teams. A private team shows its narrower setting in its own settings, and an AI answer in it comes from the allowed model.

An outside company: uses the frontier model where it's fine and keeps HR and disputes on its own model, without a policy document. Its own apps must honour the team setting, as they honour who may read the team.

What could go wrong: an app or agent that ignores the setting. It's a rule apps keep, not something the relay can stop once the words have left. And a weaker model in a narrowed team gives worse answers there.

C. Each person says which models may read their words

Like consent: every author sets their own list, and a model may read a thread only if every author in it allows that model.

You and Katerina: a setting each, and a thread goes quiet to AI the moment one cautious person joins it.

An outside company: unpredictable; whether AI works in a thread depends on who happened to write there.

What could go wrong: one setting blocks a whole team's memory, and the company's choice of models stops meaning anything.

How others do it

  • Google Workspace: an admin turns Gemini on or off per group of people, not only for the whole company.
  • Microsoft 365: a sensitivity label on a file can keep Copilot from using it.
  • Slack and Notion: an admin switch for the workspace, with the vendor's own model behind it.

Each enforces this inside one vendor's cloud. On an open protocol, nobody stands between the words and the model but the app, so the rule must be one that every app reads the same way.

Recommendation: B. The setting belongs where access already lives, on the team. That's the shape an outside company can trust: a private team is private from the models it didn't choose, too. Putting it in the protocol now means every app is built to check it from the start; adding it after A leaves apps that never look. Peek's on-device-only rule retires either way, in favour of the company's and the team's choice.

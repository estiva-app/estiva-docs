Handing a new file over

Two calls from KIN-11 (Leaf's ⌘K offers Ship's Create issue and Create project), 24 Nov 2026.

1. Who makes the file

When an app could make another app's kind of file itself, does it make it where you are, or does it always send you to the app that owns that kind?

Today

In Peek's ⌘K, Create issue and Create project make the Ship file right there, in a form the box builds, and you stay in Peek. Leaf's ⌘K offers neither: Leaf may not write Ship's files, so the box hides them.

On 19 October you decided that the app owning a "New …" declares a "create here" link, and that Peek would stop making Ship projects itself and open Ship's link instead. On 26 October you decided that a file's type is a shared meaning, and any app the workspace has added that declares the type may make its files. The two now disagree about Peek: the first sends Peek's Create project to Ship, the second lets Peek keep making it.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

It is the rule every launcher follows, ours and an outside company's: when both "make it here" and "open the owner's link" are possible, which wins. Two-way door: it is a rule in launchers and a field in an app's listing. Nothing stored on the relay depends on it; switching later changes where a button goes, not any file.

The picture

Options

A — make it where you are when you may. In Peek you pick Create project and fill the box's form without leaving the conversation, as today. In Leaf you go to Ship's form. An outside company's launcher makes the types it is allowed to and hands the rest over. What could go wrong: two forms for the same thing (the box's and Ship's), which can drift apart: Ship's form suggests the project's key, the box's form asks for it.

B — the owner's link always wins. Every Create issue, from every app, ends on Ship's own form. Peek's Create project leaves Peek. Later, Peek's permission to write Ship's projects can be withdrawn. An outside company never needs permission to write another app's types. What could go wrong: you lose your place in Peek for every new issue, which is most of what Peek's ⌘K makes today; it walks back the 26 Oct "any app that declares the type may make its files".

Do less — C: leave the rule unwritten. Peek keeps making Ship's files, Leaf opens the link, and the listing rule says nothing about which wins. Outside launchers each guess. What could go wrong: exactly that guessing, which a written rule exists to stop.

How others do it

Slack's shortcuts for other tools (Jira, Asana) open that tool's own form inside Slack, drawn from what the tool declares; Linear's and GitHub's "create issue" links prefill the owner's page. Both keep the owner in charge of the form; Slack keeps you in place. Option A is the Slack shape for apps allowed to write; B is the link shape for all.

Recommendation: A. It is what 26 October set (a type is shared, and any app that declares it may make its files), and it keeps you where you are when you can be. The link is for the app that may not. The 19 Oct record's Peek clause becomes "superseded in part".

2. What travels with you

When Leaf hands your sentence to Ship, what goes with it, and what does Ship's New issue form show?

Today

In Peek's ⌘K you type "add an issue: escape should keep the sentence" and pick Create issue: where the browser's on-device model runs, it fills the title and description from your sentence; elsewhere the form opens plain. In Ship's own ⌘K, Add issue opens Ship's New issue form empty, and your sentence stays behind in the box. The 19 Oct record passes only where to make the file, nothing you typed.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

Every app that hands over, and every app that receives, must agree on what arrives. Two-way door: a field in an app's listing; nothing on the relay depends on it. In every option the sentence travels only in the part of the link after #, which browsers never send to a server, and only to the app's own site.

Options

A — your words as typed; Ship fills its form from them, as Peek's ⌘K does. You land on Ship's New issue form with a title and description drawn from your sentence where the model runs, and your sentence as the title where it does not. Ship's own ⌘K starts doing the same, so the result is one behaviour from Peek, Leaf and Ship. An outside launcher passes one value and knows nothing of Ship's fields. What could go wrong: the model runs in Ship's tab after it opens, so the fields fill a moment after the form appears.

B — Leaf guesses the fields and sends each. The form arrives filled. But every launcher must know every app's form fields, which is the coupling a link exists to avoid, and a receiving app gets values it did not ask for. What could go wrong: an outside app renames a field and every launcher's guess lands in the wrong box.

C — do less: your words as typed become the title. Works in every browser, no model. You edit "add an issue:" out before Save. Ship's own ⌘K would do the same, so Peek fills while Leaf and Ship do not.

How others do it

Linear's and GitHub's new-issue links take named fields (title, body) — option B, which works because each is one app whose fields everyone knows. Android's "share text" hands the receiving app plain text and lets it decide — option A.

Recommendation: A. The app that owns the form owns how a sentence is read, so an outside launcher needs nothing beyond "here are the words". And you get the same filled form whether you typed in Peek, Ship or Leaf, which is the project's goal.

Who looks after a team nobody owns?

When the last owner leaves a team, who can still rename it, archive it and give people roles in it: the owners of the Company team, or the company's admins?

Today

A team's owners and admins rename it, archive it and give roles. The server refuses to let a team's last owner leave, so Miky, the only owner of Steer, QA, Backlog, Leaf, Ship and Peek, is offered Leave on none of them.

On 4 Nov you decided, in the TEA-5 review, that a last owner may leave and that the Company team's owners then look after the team ("Company owners take over"). That question was asked inline, with three options: keep Leave hidden, show it greyed, or hand the team to the longest-standing member.

The question did not mention that the protocol already has a second "company admin": the workspace's owners and admins, the roles on the server's list of who is in the company. They already delete any team, remove a pasted password for good, and are the only people who can change the company's statuses. Peek's Delete on a team already reads that role. Building the 4 Nov answer would give the protocol two different groups of company admins.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

It decides what "the company's admin" means in the protocol: every app and every outside company uses this group for every team that has no owner. Today it is a two-way door, because no team has been left ownerless and nothing new is written. It gets harder to undo once archives done by one group are stored and the two groups have drifted apart.

The picture

Under A the two arrows into a team come from two different groups. Under B both come from one. Option C instead moves the lower arrow to the upper box.

Options

A — The Company team's owners, as decided on 4 Nov (do less)

You see: Miky leaves Backlog, and he can still rename or archive it because he owns Estiva HQ. Whoever is shown as HQ's owner looks after every team nobody owns.

Outside company: learns two kinds of company admin. Team-level jobs go to the owners of the Company team. Deleting, removing a password for good, and the statuses go to the workspace admins.

What could go wrong: when HQ's last owner leaves the company, the server keeps them as HQ's owner so that HQ is never ownerless. But they can no longer write, so nobody can look after any ownerless team. A server that runs without a Company team has nobody at all.

B — The workspace's owners and admins

You see: the same as A today, because Miky is a workspace admin. The agent acting for him could also rename or archive an ownerless team, and so could the server's own admin keys, which can already delete any team.

Outside company: learns one group of company admins. It works like admins in Slack, Notion and Linear (the reason the 4 Nov record gave), and is built on the upstream rule for who is in a server's company.

What could go wrong: no screen lists the workspace admins, so "who can rename this team" is not visible anywhere. The server's own keys are included, as they already are for delete. Leaving them out would be a separate rule.

C — The Company team's owners for everything

You see: HQ's owner is the one company admin. Deleting a team, removing a password and changing the statuses move over to them too.

Outside company: one group, but an Estiva-only one, not upstream's.

What could go wrong: it rewrites three decided rules (delete, removing a password for good, the statuses). It has the same departure gap as A.

How others do it

  • Slack, Notion, Linear: the workspace's own admins manage any channel or team that has no owner. There is no "owner of the general channel" role.
  • Upstream Nostr (relay-based groups and relay membership): the relay's own member list with its roles is the authority above a group. A group's own admins sit below it.
Recommendation: B, the workspace's owners and admins. It makes one company-admin group in the protocol. That is the group that already deletes teams and keeps the statuses, and the one an outside company knows from Slack and from upstream. It still works when the Company team's owner leaves the company, and on a server without a Company team. Its cost is honesty about who is included: the agent acting for Miky, and the server's own admin keys, which can already delete teams.

Checked

  • On production, 10 Nov: Estiva HQ's only owner is Miky. The workspace list shows Miky as admin, the agent's key as admin, and two service keys as owner and admin (a relay read of the workspace list and HQ's roles).
  • No real team has no owner today; the only ownerless group is a DM.
  • Deleting a team is open to workspace owners and admins (SPEC §3.3 delete row; the relay's community steward check). The same group removes a change for good (SPEC §6.5) and writes admin-only files (record a-file-can-say-only-admins-write-it…). Peek's team Delete reads that role (folderDeletable.ts).
  • When HQ's owners all leave the company, the server keeps the first of them on HQ as owner (TEA-7, sync_company_roster).
  • The server already refuses a leave of the Company team (probed on production as QA-1: company-team-kept).
  • Not verified: whether any app plans to show the workspace's admins.

Decision brief · Teams and following · 9 Nov 2026

Who is in the company

When our server lets a key in, is it one of the company's people, so it sits on the Company team and in everyone's people lists? Or are some keys let in only to do a job, without being part of the company?

Today

Estiva HQ is already marked as the Company team, and nobody can delete or archive it. On 2 Nov you decided that everyone in the company is on its member list, and the server keeps them there: it adds each person it lets in, and nobody can leave. Its chat becomes #general. That decision assumed that everyone the server lets in is a person. On production that is not true.

The server lets in 14 keys today:

  • People: Miky, Katerina, QA-1, QA-2, QA-3 and "test". These are accounts someone signs in with, so they count as people under every option below.
  • Agents: Claude (steered by Miky), Claude (steered by Katerina), PC (steered by Katerina). Agents are members like anyone and show under their own heading.
  • Five keys that are not anyone: the server's owner key (never used), "Relay Operator" (last active 24 Sep), and three app keys that have each only published their app's listing (Ship's is one). An app key has to be let in, because the server lets only keys it has let in write anything.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

It decides what "everyone in the company" means: for Estiva HQ's people, for every people picker in Peek, Ship and Leaf, for a later "@company" mention, and for an outside company that runs bots and integrations on its own server.

Two-way door. The server can later put a key on the list or take it off, and the event history shows it either way. What sticks is how apps handle it: if the list includes keys that are not people, each app starts hiding them its own way, and those filters are hard to take back.

The picture

Options

A · Every key the server lets in is in the company do less

What you see: Katerina opens Estiva HQ and sees Miky, herself and the QA accounts, plus three keys with no name, an unused owner key and "Relay Operator". Ship offers the same keys as assignees.

Outside company: its Company team lists every integration and bot next to its staff.

What could go wrong: each app hides those keys its own way ("no profile" in Peek today), which is the second meaning of member the 2 Nov record turned down. A future app that does not filter shows them all.

B · Letting a key in says whether it is someone or a service recommended

What you see: Estiva HQ lists Miky, Katerina, the QA accounts and "test", and the three agents under their own heading. App and server keys still work and appear nowhere.

Outside company: one clear idea. A service is let in to write but is not part of the company. Whoever lets a key in already knows which it is, so no app has to guess. The server cannot be asked to add a service to the Company team.

What could go wrong: a key marked the wrong way, such as a person marked as a service, is left out of #general until someone fixes the mark. The five existing keys are marked once, by whoever runs the server, when it is upgraded. That is the same one-time step that named Estiva HQ the Company team.

C · Services are not let in at all

What you see: the same as B.

Outside company: it has to give its bots a second way to write, through server settings instead of the member list. That departs from the Nostr standard for member lists, where being on the list is what lets a key write.

What could go wrong: every app's listing key is set up differently from everything else, and the spec's rule "an app's key is let in like a member" is reversed.

How others do it

  • Slack: apps are bot users, not members. #general holds every full member, and a bot is in a channel only if someone adds it.
  • Google Workspace: service accounts belong to the project, not the directory. They appear in no people list.
  • Linear: integrations act as app users that are kept out of team members.
  • Nostr's member-list standard has no such distinction. Every key on the list is equal, which is why A is what falls out by default.

Recommendation

B. Whether a key is one of the company's people is a fact only the side letting it in knows, so the server should hold it once for every app and every outside company, rather than each app guessing from a missing profile. It keeps your 2 Nov rule, one meaning of member, without stretching "the company" to cover machines. C gets the same list by breaking the standard way keys are let in to write.

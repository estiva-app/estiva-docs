Arranging a topic's files

For Miky. This writes a new kind of thing to the relay, so on your answer it becomes a decision record in estiva-docs, and your merge accepts it.

When someone drags a file to a new place in a topic, or switches it between a row and a card, what does the topic keep, and what happens when two people arrange the same topic at the same moment?

Settled already, by you on 6 Nov: a topic's files keep one order and one look (row or card), kept on the topic, the same for everyone, and anyone who can add a file to the topic can arrange it. This brief is only about how that is kept.

Today

A topic keeps its list of files as one small entry per file: "Testing Widgets is on this topic", "Widgets 2 is on this topic", each signed by whoever added it (your call of 30 Oct, a topic keeps its own list). Nobody can arrange the files. The protocol says plainly that no order is promised: each app sorts them however its design says, and nothing records a row or a card.

Why it reaches beyond this project

  • It is the first order a person sets by hand anywhere in the protocol. Every list so far is sorted by time or title. Whatever we choose here is what the next hand-ordered list copies: a team's pinned files, an initiative's projects, an outside company's own collection.
  • Anyone in the team arranges, not one owner. So two people moving files at once is the normal case, not an odd one. The rule for it is the rule every outside app has to follow.
  • A row or a card becomes something the topic says, not something each app decides. Every app that draws a topic's files learns to read it.
Mostly a two-way door. Nothing is arranged today, so nothing migrates. An arrangement is a preference: if we switch shape later, apps fall back to "order added" and someone drags again. What is harder to undo is the rule outside apps have learned, and, for option B, history lines that stay on the relay forever.

The options

All three use the same example: on the Widgets topic, in the same minute, you drag Widgets 2 to the top while Katerina makes Testing Widgets a card.

A. Each file's place and look are their own small notes on the topic recommended

The topic keeps, per file, a "place" note and a "look" note, beside the entry that lists it. A move writes one note for the file that moved, saying "between these two". Changing the look writes one note for that file. Nothing else is touched.

What you and Katerina see. Both changes stand, in every app, with no warning. If you both move the same file, the later move wins, as for any other field today. History can say "Miky moved Widgets 2" apart from "Miky added Widgets 2".

What an outside company gets. Two more notes per file, read like the list entry it already reads. An app that does not know them shows files in the order they were added, which is today's behaviour.

What could go wrong.

  • A file taken off and put back comes back at the end, not where it was: a place older than the file's latest "added" is ignored. That is on purpose, but someone may expect the old place.
  • Two people dropping two different files into the same gap at once both stand, and the two land side by side in a fixed order. Neither is lost.
  • A topic with files you cannot read: their places exist but are skipped, so you see the rest in the right order. Nothing new is revealed, since the list entry already names the file.

B. The place and look are written on the file's list entry

No new notes. Moving a file, or changing its look, writes the file's list entry again, now saying "on this topic, first, as a card".

What you and Katerina see. The same result as A in this example. But every drag looks like "added to Widgets" to anything that reads entries: history, and "which topics is this file in".

What an outside company gets. Nothing new to read, but the list entry, which today means only "on" or "off", now also carries a place and a look, and an app has to tell a move from an add.

What could go wrong.

  • A stale tab puts a removed file back. You take Roadmap off the topic. Katerina, in a tab that has not caught up, drags Roadmap one place down. Her app is told it is out of date and, by today's rule for any field but the text, sends her change again. Her change says "Roadmap is on the list", so Roadmap is back. Avoiding it needs a special case in every app.
  • History and Activity fill with "added" lines for files that were only moved, and those stay on the relay.

C. One arrangement for the whole topic do less

The topic keeps one note: the whole order, with each file's look. Every drag saves the whole arrangement again. This is the simplest to build and to read.

What you and Katerina see. Arranging alone works. Arranging together, the later save wins whole: your move disappears and nobody is told. Keeping both would need a new merge rule, like the one the text has, for every app.

What an outside company gets. One value to read, a list. To write it safely it must merge someone else's arrangement into its own, or lose theirs.

What could go wrong. Beside the lost move: the arrangement goes stale. A file added after the last arrange is not in it and falls to the end; a file taken off stays in it until someone arranges again. It is the same reason the topic's list itself was made one entry per file and not one list, on 30 Oct.

How others do it

  • Trello and Linear: each card or issue keeps its own position number; moving one writes only that one. This is A.
  • Figma: each layer keeps a key "between its neighbours", so two people reordering different layers never collide. The same key A uses.
  • Notion: a page keeps its children as one ordered list, and Notion's own server merges two people's edits. On an open relay there is no server to merge for us, which is C's problem.
  • Nostr lists (NIP-51): one author's whole list, saved whole. That fits one owner, not a team, and is why a topic's list is one entry per file.

Recommendation

A: each file's place and look are their own notes on the topic. You decided anyone who can add a file can arrange, so two people arranging at once is normal, and each person's change must touch only what they changed. A carries the reasoning behind the topic's list one step further, keeps "added" and "moved" as different acts in history, and can never put back a file someone took off. An outside app learns two more notes of a kind it already reads. C is the "do less", and loses a person's change without telling anyone; B looks smaller but makes the list entry mean three things.

Following a topic

For Miky. It changes how the Accepted record of 30 Sep, "membership replaces following", applies to topics, so its record is written as Proposed and waits on your merge.

Answered with none of these options (30 Oct, evening). Miky couldn't decide following on its own, so it was walked through the scenarios in his Leaf page "Read / Unread notifications + Activity". He answered with his own model: a team has members and followers; a topic or space is followed at All, at Important, or not at all; who is in a team and who follows a place at which level is visible. It is recorded in a-team-has-members-and-followers-and-a-place-is-followed-at-a-level. Option A's membership triggers contradict his scenarios 5.1 and 5.2, and its private All contradicts his answer.

When Katerina follows the Widgets topic and picks All, so she hears about every file it lists, where does that choice live, and who can see it?

Today

You are told about a file's conversation when you are a member of the file: you made it, wrote in it, were mentioned in it or assigned to it, or were added. Anyone who can read the file sees its members. Only muting is private, kept in one list that every app reads. Peek's old private "following" list was retired on 30 Sep, your call in CON-19, because Ship couldn't see it: reading a file in Ship never cleared Peek's dot, and one mention lit a file's whole history.

On 29 Oct you decided that a topic is a file, and that its icon says how you follow it: Important (dashed circle, the default) or All (full circle). Nothing says yet where that choice lives, or how Ship and Leaf learn it. A topic made in Peek today is already a file whose conversation is comments, so membership already decides whose dot it lights. Older topics are channels, which light only for a mention.

Why it reaches beyond this page

  • One rule for "is this unread for you". Today every app, ours or an outside company's, answers it with one sentence: you are a member. All adds a second path, and every app has to compute it the same way. If they don't, Ship and Peek disagree again, which is the bug that retired following.
  • What is public. Either anyone can see who follows a topic (A, C), or nobody can (B). That is a privacy call for every company on the protocol. It also decides whether an agent, or a server that later sends email and phone notifications, can know whom to tell.
  • The Accepted record of 30 Sep. A adds one path to it: a file's conversation reaches you when you are its member or a topic you follow at All lists it. B brings back a private following list, the alternative that record turned down, with one new fact: every app now reads it. C leaves the record as it is.

All three options are two-way doors. What is new lives either in each person's own private list, which can be reshaped at any time, or in membership events that have been public since 30 Sep. The one-way option, where All makes you a member of every file, is set aside at the end.

Options

A · Following is being a member; All is yours alone recommended

Following a topic is being its member, by the same triggers as any file, and Unfollow is leaving it, so the topic's members are its followers. Important is the default. All is a private setting, kept like muting in one list that every app reads. With All, a new message on a file the topic lists is unread for you once it is newer than both your choice and the file's listing. It does not make you that file's member.

Katerina sees
She clicks Widgets' dashed circle and it fills. A comment on Widgets 2: build 1, an issue she has never touched, lights Widgets blue in Peek and lands in her Screener. She reads it in Ship, and the dot clears in Peek.
Miky sees
Widgets shows Katerina as following it. The members of build 1 are still him and the agent. He can't see that she chose All.
Outside company
Reads members as it already does, and reads which topics list a file (decided 30 Oct). For its own user it also reads one more private list, the same way it reads that user's mutes.
Could go wrong
A file in two topics you follow at All lights both, and reading it once clears both. Muting a file still wins over All. A file taken out of the topic stops reaching you. If two of your devices change different topics in the same second, the later list wins and the other change is lost, as with muting today.

B · Following is a private list of its own

Which topics you follow, and at which level, live only in your own private list, read by every app. That fixes the reason following was retired. Nobody else can see who follows a topic. Membership stays as it is beside it, so writing in a topic still makes you its member.

Katerina sees
Following Widgets at All works as in A, but nobody can see that she follows it. If she once wrote in Widgets she is also its member, so after Unfollow its own talk still lights her dot until she leaves it too: two switches for one place.
Miky sees
Widgets shows no followers. Before he posts, he can tell who can read it, never who will hear about it.
Outside company
Can't know who follows a topic. A server that sends email or phone notifications can't work out whom to tell, so each device has to tell it what its person follows.
Could go wrong
"I unfollowed, so why does it still light?" Every app needs both ideas in its settings. Its record has to say why the list the 30 Sep record turned down is now right: every app reads it.

C · Do less: All is only a way of looking

Nothing new is stored. Following a topic is being its member, as in A. The full circle opens the topic on All and changes no dot. A file's talk reaches you only when you are a member of that file, exactly as the 30 Sep record says.

Katerina sees
The full circle opens Widgets on All, and that is all it does. A comment on build 1 gives her no dot and no Screener row unless she is a member of build 1. She sees it when she looks at Widgets.
Miky sees
The same as today: an issue's members are the people involved in it.
Outside company
Nothing new to read or write.
Could go wrong
The icon looks like a notification setting and isn't one. Katerina, working on Widgets, misses a question on a Widgets issue where nobody mentioned her, the very case your 29 Oct dot was for. C can become A later without undoing anything.

Set aside: All makes you a member of every file. Picking All would write you in as a member of each file the topic lists, so no new rule is needed. It fails three ways. Katerina's name appears on every Widgets issue, so "members" stops meaning the people involved. A file added while her laptop is closed misses its first comments, because she joins only when her app next runs. And going back to Important can't tell which files she joined herself. It is also the one-way door: those membership events stay on the relay.

How others do it

  • Slack: who has joined a channel is public, its member list. How loud it is (all, mentions, nothing) is private to you. That is the shape of A.
  • GitHub: who watches a repository is public. "Participating" is like our membership; "All activity" tells you about every issue without subscribing you to each one. Also A.
  • Matrix, an open protocol like ours: room membership is public; notification rules are the user's own private data. Also A. One thing breaks for us: Matrix's server holds those rules and can push from them, while our private list is encrypted to the person, so a phone or email notifier can't read it without the person's device telling it.

Recommendation

A: following a topic is being its member, and All is a private setting every app reads. For the protocol, one relation, membership, means "I'm in this place" for files and topics alike, and the only new thing is a private list of the same kind as mutes. No app has to be online to write anything for All to work, which is what "100% reliable" needs. For the person, Katerina hears about everything in Widgets without her name appearing on issues she has no part in. Miky can see who follows a topic, and how loud each person set it stays their own. Cost doesn't tip it.

What it leaves open

  • Teams. Your notes say that following a team shows its public spaces in your sidebar, and that a team's members follow its public spaces by default. That's the next brief, built on this answer.
  • Writing once follows. Under every option, writing in a topic makes you its member, as it does on a file today. So reporting one bug in #bugs makes #bugs light up for you until you unfollow it. That's worth its own question, together with front doors.
  • Older topics that are channels keep "unread only for a mention" until they become files.

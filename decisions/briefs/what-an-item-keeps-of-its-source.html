What an item keeps

When you mark Katerina’s words as an Insight, a Journal entry or a Risk, does the item keep its own copy of her words, or show them from where she wrote them? And who makes sure an item never shows words to someone who could not read where they were written?

Today

No app offers Mark yet. What is decided: an item is its own file, filed in one team whose people read it, about an issue, a project or an Initiative (the last part waits for your merge of estiva-docs#447). It names its sources, the messages and blocks it came from, and its readers are fixed when it is made. Two things were left open on purpose: whether an item copies its source’s words, and an item whose source has other readers than its team. Both were parked “for the brainstorm on private teams and notes”.

Today every team is open to the whole company: no team can be made private yet. So the only source with other readers is a direct message; a person’s notes come later. Earlier today you decided that the project offers Mark only where the source’s readers are the item’s team’s readers, and that your own mark is accepted at once, except a Decision naming someone else as the decider.

One rule is already in the protocol for a close cousin: when a message shows a block of a Leaf page, it shows the block live and must never store its words, “because a copy goes stale and keeps showing text to someone who has since lost access”. That was decided on 7 October.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

  • It sets what an item is for every app and every outside company: a snapshot of someone’s words, or a window onto them. An agent that reads a project’s Journal as its memory reads whichever we choose.
  • It answers part of an Accepted record (an item’s readers are fixed when it is made): its “whether an item copies its source’s words” line, and, if the relay checks it, its “source with other readers” line, which gets a rule instead of a wait.
  • “Mark only where readers match” lives in our apps only. Any other client, or an agent, could write an item in a team that points at a direct message and repeats its words. Only the relay can make the rule hold for everyone.

One-way door for a copy. Words copied into an item are on the relay for good: every save of a file is kept, so even rewriting the item leaves them in its history. They stay readable by the item’s team after the source’s team turns private, and undoing that means deleting a version in every item. Showing words from their source, live or pinned, is two-way: nothing is copied, so we could still start copying later. A relay check is cheapest to add now, while no item exists on the relay; added later, it cannot touch the items already written.

The picture: copy, live, pinned

Your real example: Katerina’s 18 August message in Universal Navigation, “one search, an All topics switch”, marked as an Insight. Three moments: you mark it; she later edits her message; later still, private teams exist and her team turns private. The last column is what someone sees who reads the Insight’s team but not hers.

Options: what an item keeps

A · Copy the words into the item do less · upstream’s way

What you see: you mark her message; the Insight holds her words as its own text, readable without fetching anything. If she later fixes a typo or changes her mind, the Insight still shows what she first wrote, with no hint.

Outside company: the simplest thing to read; any Nostr client draws it, as upstream highlights do.

Locks in: her words in every item’s history, for good.

What could go wrong: when private teams arrive and her team turns private, everyone in the Insight’s team still reads her words. It contradicts the protocol’s own “pointer, never copy” rule for a shown block. An agent keeps quoting words their author corrected.

B · Show her words live, copy nothing

What you see: the Insight shows her message as it reads now. Anyone who cannot read her message sees “not available to you”, because the relay simply does not hand it to them.

Outside company: reads the source to draw the item, the way a shown block works today.

What could go wrong: an accepted item’s words can change after it was accepted. Your 8 September Decision, “ditch that idea completely”, is your own message; edit it later and the Journal entry reads otherwise, with nothing on the entry to say so. A deleted source leaves the item with no words.

C · Copy only the title

What you see: the title is stored, from her words or typed by you; the body is live as in B.

What could go wrong: both problems at once, smaller: the title still repeats her words to whoever reads the item, and the body still drifts with her edits. It is listed because the title is the part every list shows, and it is where the question below comes back.

D · Show her words as they were when marked, checked at every read recommended

What you see: the Insight shows her message as it read when you marked it. If she has edited it since, a line says “edited since marked”, and her current words are one tap away. A Leaf or Ship block shows as it was in the save of the page you marked it on; every save is already kept, which is what the History panel opens. Anyone who cannot read her message, or the page, sees “not available to you”.

Outside company: reads the source and the one version the item names, one extra read; it gets a fixed record of what was decided, like a link to a file at a given commit.

Locks in: that an item points, never copies; a later “copy” option stays possible.

What could go wrong: if she deletes her message, or someone deletes that version of a page (as for a pasted password), the item shows it was deleted, and a Decision loses its words. A list of items costs a read of their sources; not measured yet on your workspace. The title is still stored, below.

Is a suggested title a copy? Yes. Whatever the title says, suggested or typed, is stored in the item under your name and readable by its team for good. D keeps her words out of the item; it cannot keep them out of a title. Two ways to start:

  • The title starts filled with a short suggestion you edit. One step, and Mark stays as quick as “I will do it for her”. Because Mark is offered only where readers match, the title tells nobody anything they could not read when you marked it. It stays readable if her team later turns private, as anything you wrote about it in a message would.
  • The title starts empty and you write it. Nothing of hers is copied unless you choose to write it; one more thing to type each time.

Lean: starts filled, and the hint says plainly that it is kept. What happens to such titles when a team turns private is for the private-teams brainstorm.

Who checks it

Separate from what an item keeps, and it holds whichever option you pick: with A it is the only guard; with D it guards the title and the pointer.

  • R · The relay refuses an item whose source lives where not everyone who reads the item can read it. Today that means a direct message, so a DM message cannot be marked at all, by any app. The direction matters: a later private team may mark an open team’s message, never the other way round. Any outside company’s app gets the same answer from the relay, so “readers match” is a protocol rule, not our apps’ promise. The relay already does this kind of check: it refuses an edit whose team is not the edited message’s team.
  • P · Our apps only. Peek, Ship and Leaf never offer Mark where readers differ; anything else may write such an item. Under D the source then shows “not available”, and the title shows to the whole team.

How others do it

  • Upstream Nostr highlights (NIP-84) copy the highlighted text into the highlight. That suits public notes; on a company relay with private places it is option A’s leak.
  • A code host’s permalink shows a file’s lines as they were at one commit, with the newest version a click away, and only to people who can open the repository: option D’s shape.
  • Our own shown block (a message showing a Leaf block) is option B, by the 7 October decision, and its reason is the one against A.

Recommendation

D, checked by the relay (R), with the title starting filled and saying it is kept. An item is the team’s memory of what was learned and decided, so it must say exactly what was marked and accepted, even after its author edits it; only D does that. It must also never be the thing that carries words past the people allowed to read them; D copies nothing, and the relay makes “readers match” hold for every app and agent, not only ours. It keeps the protocol’s one rule for a shown part, pointer never copy, and adds one thing: which version. Cost does not tip it: D is a medium build and the relay rule is cheapest now, before any item is on the relay.

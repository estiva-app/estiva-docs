Whose space is it

Is "space" something about the topic, so it stays a space when it moves to another team, or the team's own choice, so the team it moves to decides again?

You decided today that a team holds three kinds of place: its chat, its spaces ("topics the team marks"), and the topics it leads, and that a team's members and followers follow its spaces at Important. You also decided just now that a topic lives in exactly one team, and only a move changes which. The first version of this brief rested on a topic in two teams; that case is gone. What is left is where the mark is kept, which decides two things: what a move does, and who may mark.

Today

Nothing is a space yet. Every topic is stored in the team it was created in, for good: that is who may read it and write on it. Which team shows it is the team's list, and a move changes only that. So after a move, a topic is shown by one team and stored in another. Today both are open to everyone in the company, so nobody notices.

Who may change a team's list today: anyone who may write in that team, which for all 9 teams is everyone in the company.

Why it reaches beyond this stage

A space is how one act puts a place on many people's sidebars: every member and every follower of the team, at Important. This sets, for every app and every company that adopts the protocol, who may do that to a team's people: anyone who may write on the topic, or the team itself. Today the two are the same people. They part when private teams come, the next stage: a topic stored in an open team can be shown by a private one, as a private team's public front door, and the other way round after a move.

Undo. Both can be changed later by rewriting each mark into the other shape, since a topic is now in one team. A's marks, though, say nothing about which team made them, so after moves a rewrite has to guess; C's always name their team.

Options

A · A space is about the topic, and goes with it do less

What you see. Katerina moves #bug to Design, and it shows in Design's section as a space at once: everyone in or following Design follows it at Important from that moment. A space is a permanent place wherever it is, which matches your 9 Oct split of permanent topics from project-like and short-term ones.

Outside company. Simplest to build and to explain: one more fact on the topic, beside its title.

What could go wrong. Moving a space quietly pushes it onto everyone in the new team, without anyone in that team choosing it. And who may mark it is whoever may write where it is stored, not the team that shows it: after a move, someone outside Design can mark or unmark one of Design's spaces, and once Design is private, someone in Design may be unable to. Each app also has to read every topic's history to know a team's spaces.

C · A space is the team's own mark on its list recommended

What you see. The move takes Engineering's mark off with the topic. In the move dialog, "Keep it a space in Design" is ticked when #bug was a space, so in the usual case it looks just like A: one act, and it is a space in Design. The difference is that it is Design's mark, made by someone allowed to make it.

Outside company. One rule: a team's spaces are what that team says on its own list, which every app already reads to draw the team. Who may mark is checked against the team that shows it, and that holds when teams become private and when a team is shared with another company.

What could go wrong. A move by an app that does not offer the tick (an older app, the CLI) drops the mark, and #bug becomes a topic Design leads until someone marks it again. Who may mark is still open: today anyone who may write in a team may change its list, so for our open teams that is everyone in the company; C lets the relay narrow it to the team's members, which is part of the next batch of questions.

Dropped: the mark on the topic counting only for the team it is stored in. After a move, the team storing #bug no longer shows it, so the mark would count for nobody and Design could never make it a space.

How others do it

  • Matrix: a space lists its rooms in the space itself, with a "suggested" flag on that entry: the space's choice, not the room's. That is C.
  • Slack: default channels are a workspace setting, not a property of the channel. C-like.
  • Discord: each server's onboarding picks its default channels. C-like.
  • Linear: moving an issue to another team asks you to map its status and labels to the new team's: the receiving team's settings apply, as in C with its tick.

Recommendation

C: a space is the team's own mark on its list. "Topics the team marks" is a team's statement, so the team should be the one allowed to make it, and that only stays true under C once teams can be private. With the tick in the move dialog, a person sees what A gives in the usual case, while a move never pushes a space onto a team that did not say yes. The cost is a relay change to the team's list; that is a build cost, and it does not change the shape.

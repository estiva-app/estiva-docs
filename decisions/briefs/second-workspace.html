When a second company gets a workspace, how do its people reach it?

A brief for Miky · 26 Oct 2026 · Slack Connect stage 1, "Estiva ID serves more than one workspace, and each workspace signs with its own key" · one main call, then two smaller ones it brings

The question

When Estiva's deployment holds a second company's workspace, does each workspace get its own web address for Peek, Ship and Leaf, or is there one Peek where you switch between workspaces? And then: is one sign-in good for every workspace you belong to, or only the one you signed in to?

The example throughout: a company called Acme gets its own workspace. Alice works at Acme. You have not picked the real company yet, so this is the shape, not a moment that happened.

Today

  • There is one workspace, Estiva's. Its relay is at estiva.estiva.app. Peek, Ship and Leaf are each built with that one address inside them, and nobody can point them anywhere else.
  • One relay deployment can already hold several workspaces. It tells them apart by the address you connect to, and each has its own roster. But it signs everything (each Folder's record and listing, who is in, the system messages) with one key for the whole deployment.
  • Estiva ID signs everyone in at id.estiva.app, and lets new people into Estiva's workspace only. A sign-in names who you are and which app you are using, never a workspace. Handles are already kept per workspace.
  • Decided before: the workspace is the relay, and an app treats its configured relay as the edge of the workspace, never a preference you change. You accepted today that apps stay on one relay, and that a partner is a second workspace on Estiva's deployment for now.

Why it reaches beyond this ticket

  • It sets what every app, including an outside company's, must know. Either an app still serves exactly one workspace, as the Specification says now, or every app learns to hold several workspaces and switch between them.
  • It decides what a link to a file looks like. Today a link names the app's address and the file. A second workspace has to show up somewhere: in the address, in the path, or nowhere.
  • It decides how far a stolen sign-in reaches. Today it would reach every workspace the person is in, once there is more than one.
  • Doors. Links are kept as written, in messages and descriptions on the relay. Whatever shape a link to Acme's file takes, it has to keep opening for good, so changing the shape later means a redirect that stays forever. That is cheap but permanent. The sign-in call is a two-way door: no saved data names it. Each workspace's own key cannot be changed once its Folders exist, because a Folder's address names that key. So Estiva's workspace keeps today's key, and nothing anyone has seen changes.

The options

A · Do less: each workspace has its own address, and each copy of an app serves one workspace

What you and Katerina see. Nothing changes at peek.estiva.app. Alice opens peek.acme.estiva.app, taps the same kind of passkey at Estiva ID, and sees Acme's Folders only. If you were in Acme too, you would keep a second tab or bookmark, the way people keep acme.atlassian.net. A link to an Acme file is peek.acme.estiva.app/topic/…: the address says which workspace it belongs to.

What an outside company gets. Its apps need nothing new: an app still serves the one relay it was set up with. When a company later runs its own relay on its own domain, as in chat.acme.com, the shape is the same.

What it locks in. One workspace per copy of an app. A switcher can come later as a list of your workspaces that opens each one's address, without any app holding two workspaces at once.

What could go wrong. Nobody in two workspaces sees unread from the other one without opening its tab. A link pasted from Estiva's Peek into Acme's opens Estiva's address, which Alice cannot read. Under the rule that a pasted link is that file on any Estiva app's address, her Peek tries its own relay first, and that only finds the file once it lives in a Folder shared between the two companies (stage 2). Each new workspace needs its addresses set up on Estiva's side.

B · One address for each app, the workspace in the link, and a switcher

What you and Katerina see. One Peek tab. If you are in Acme too, a switcher, Slack-style, perhaps with a dot when the other workspace has something new. Links gain a workspace part, peek.estiva.app/acme/topic/…. Every link already sent has no such part, so it means "Estiva's workspace" for good.

What an outside company gets. Every app learns to hold several workspaces: a list of them from the identity service (a new part of the protocol for every identity service), its own relay connection per workspace, and what it keeps on the device kept apart per workspace, with sign-out per workspace. The rule that an app's relay is fixed configuration becomes a choice at runtime.

What it locks in. Apps that span workspaces, which is the multi-relay app you set aside for companies today, in a narrower form.

What could go wrong. A bug that mixes the two workspaces' device copies shows Acme's text in Estiva's view. Every app's paths change shape, and the record that a file's address is the same in every app, with only the host changed, gains a rule for the missing part. Katerina designs a switcher in three apps.

C · One address, and the sign-in picks the workspace

What you and Katerina see. If you are in both, Estiva ID asks which workspace when you sign in, and the app stays in that one until you sign in again. A link looks like today's, so a link to an Acme file opened while you are in Estiva shows "not found".

What an outside company gets. Its apps take the relay from the sign-in answer instead of their setup, which is a new part of the protocol, and must still keep each workspace's device copy apart.

What could go wrong. Links stop saying where they belong, which is exactly what stage 2's shared Folders need them to say.

The two calls it brings

2 · Is a sign-in good for one workspace, or for all of yours?

  • One workspace (recommended). Signing in to Acme's Peek gives a sign-in for Acme only. Estiva ID refuses to sign anything that would open a connection to another workspace's relay, and the relay already refuses an event unless the connection is signed in as its author. So a sign-in stolen from one workspace's app cannot read or write in another. You still tap your passkey once: the second workspace's sign-in happens without a prompt. An outside app adds one standard sign-in parameter that names the relay. Two-way door: nothing saved depends on it.
  • All of yours. As today: a sign-in names you and the app. Any copy of Peek can open a connection as you to any workspace you are in, and each relay's roster is the only wall.

3 · Is your name and picture the same in every workspace?

  • The same everywhere (recommended). You set your name and picture once, in Estiva ID, and every workspace you are in shows them. Only the handle differs (miky@estiva.estiva.app, miky@acme.estiva.app), as Estiva ID already stores it. Two-way door: a per-workspace name can be added later.
  • Per workspace (Slack). Each workspace has its own name and picture for you. More to edit and keep, and Estiva ID's profile screen gains a workspace choice.

In every option, Acme's people can see that the same key is in both workspaces when someone belongs to both: a person is one key everywhere, as decided before. Nothing here hides that.

How others do it

  • Atlassian, Zendesk: each customer has its own address (acme.atlassian.net), with one Atlassian account across all of them. This is option A.
  • Slack: you sign in at acme.slack.com, the web app runs at one address with the workspace in the path, and a sidebar switches between workspaces. Each workspace gets its own sign-in token. This is B's look, with call 2's "one workspace" sign-in.
  • Linear, Notion: one address, the workspace in the path, and a switcher. This is option B.
  • What breaks for us: those companies write every client themselves. On an open protocol, B and C make every outside app span workspaces. A asks nothing of them.

Recommendation

A, each workspace with its own address, with a sign-in good for one workspace, and one name and picture everywhere.

The reasons are the protocol's shape and the person. A keeps what you decided today: an app serves one relay, and talking across companies is the relays' job. An outside company's app needs nothing new, and a company running its own relay later looks exactly the same. For the person: most people are in one workspace, and with shared Folders in stage 2 nobody has to join a partner's workspace to work with them. The switcher serves the few who belong to two companies, and it can come later as a list of addresses. Binding a sign-in to one workspace keeps a leak in one company's app from reaching another company. The cost is plain: someone in two workspaces keeps two tabs.

  • Peek, Ship and Leaf are each built with https://estiva.estiva.app as their relay: peek-app .github/workflows/deploy.yml:160, leaf deploy.yml:106, estiva-ship deploy.yml:216.
  • The relay picks the workspace by the address it is reached at, and fails closed for an unknown one (buzz crates/buzz-relay/src/tenant.rs:76-95). It checks a sign-in to the connection against that same address (api/bridge.rs:225-232). It refuses an event whose author is not the signed-in key (handlers/event.rs:662).
  • The relay signs every workspace with one key from its environment (crates/buzz-relay/src/main.rs:38-46). Its per-workspace key column, communities.signing_key (migrations/0001_initial_schema.sql:53-58), is never read or set. Its design notes make it the per-workspace key (docs/multi-tenant-relay.md:907-908).
  • A Folder's address names the relay's key, and an app keeps its record only when that key signed it (Specification §3.2). So Estiva keeps today's key.
  • An Estiva ID sign-in names the person and the app, and has one issuer (estiva-id src/domain/jwt.ts:148-179). Which relays an app may sign a connection for is one list for the whole deployment (src/config.ts:175). Handles are kept per workspace (migrations/002_profile.sql:38-67). Letting people in, the admin flag, invites and the one operator identity all assume one workspace (src/config.ts:184-222).
  • Estiva ID's own plan is one sign-in address and a workspace address per customer (docs/OPEN-SEAMS.md, "Consequence to act on now" and "Multi-workspace").

## Who it affects

Claude writes Decided lines on Ship issues and decision records in Miky's
name today, each saying where he gave the answer; he reads them when he
chooses. As Journal entries, each reads "Decided by Miky · written by Claude",
and Miky is told once that he was named, so a decision put in his name that he
did not make is caught by him, not by whoever reads it later. The same holds
for Katerina, named as the decider of how a screen looks.

## Decision

- **A Journal entry shows "Decided by X · written by Y".** Y is whoever signed
  the entry.
- **The person named as the decider is told once**, when they are named, the
  way a mention tells them, so they can correct it.
- **Anyone may still accept the entry**, under the app's, company's or team's
  rule. Being named as the decider does not make someone the only one who
  accepts.
- **This keeps the intelligence record.** Being named as the decider is being
  named: an agent that writes an entry in someone's name reaches them this way,
  under their own following and levels, and in no other way.

## Rejected alternatives

- **Only the decider accepts the entry.** Turned down: it stalls whenever that
  person is away, and it would set a rule on who accepts, which each company
  sets for itself.
- **Show the decider with no notice.** Turned down: a decision put in someone's
  name that they never see stands unchallenged, and agents read the Journal as
  memory.

## Consequences

- A wrong "Decided by" is caught by the person it names; correcting it is a
  change kept in the entry's history.
- An agent that writes many entries naming a person adds a notice for each;
  how often an agent may name someone is open, with loops.
- How the notice draws, on the Desk and in the Activity indicator, is shaped
  with the build.

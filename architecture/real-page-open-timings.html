Real page-open timings

Shaped, 2026-11-19 · Ship project ·builds on Production errors reach us · Goal: per kind of screen, we know how long Miky and Katerina each really waited for Peek, Ship and Leaf to open.

Today a page's speed is measured only by the speed kit: a QA account, or Claude signed in as Miky, opening a few fixed pages from one machine. Nothing measures the opens a person really makes. This project adds a timer beside the error reporter. It times every open, sends a short timing down the same road as an error report, and Claude prints what each of us waited. Look at the Map first, then at what a timing may contain.

A timing may contain: the app and its version; the kind of screen (never its address); how it was opened (a new tab, a reload, a click inside the app); how long until the page was ready, and in Peek how long until its saved picture showed; when; the browser; the person's public key; whether it is a person, a QA account or an agent.Miky, in Sentry. Claude, reading. Sentry the company, as the host.Nobody. Sentry deletes it after 30 days.The timer in the person's browser, then Estiva ID's server before it passes the timing on. Tested by planting text and reading what leaves the browser and what leaves our server.
A timing never contains: titles, file names, page addresses, which issue, page or conversation was opened, message or document text, what the relay was asked, or the person's IP address.Nobody outside the browser.—The same two checks and the same test.
What the timings add up to: a log of when each person opened which kind of screen, and how long it took. Miky chose to keep the person's key on it (19 Nov), because "what we each wait" cannot be answered without it.As the first row.—Katerina is told before the first app sends one.
Who can send a timing. A signed-in person's app, through Estiva ID's server; the public key on it is the one signed in. Nothing is sent before sign-in: there is no door for a signed-out timing.—Sentry's address is held by our servers only.Estiva ID's server.
Timings cannot crowd out errors. They are counted under their own limit, in the browser and on the server, apart from the error allowance.——Estiva ID's server. Tested by sending many timings, then an error.

Read on 2026-11-19 from origin/main of each repository, and from the Sentry account.

  • The error reporter is packages/platform/src/error-reports.ts in estiva-foundation, published as @estiva-app/platform 0.9.0. Peek, Ship and Leaf each start it in src/main.tsx and tell it the kind of screen on every navigation.
  • Estiva ID's src/domain/error-reports.ts builds every report again from its own list and sends Sentry one error event. A field it does not name is dropped, so a timing cannot pass today. Its limits count per person and per app (30 in ten minutes, 100 an hour).
  • No app marks a page as ready. Loading placeholders are marked in each app (PageSkeleton in Ship and Leaf, aria-busy and skeleton classes in all three). Peek notes when its saved picture showed and when the live page replaced it (src/lib/pagePicture.ts).
  • The speed kit (scripts/app-speed.mjs in Peek's repository) calls a page ready when its awaited text is on screen and no placeholder is left outside the sidebar. The awaited text is a test fixture; the placeholder check is what a real tab can run.
  • Sentry: the account is on the free plan, which includes 5,000,000 spans a month, and took 81 errors in the last 30 days.
  • A timing sent by hand to the Ship project through the envelope address Estiva ID's server already uses was stored as a span, counted apart from errors, and read back with Claude's read-only token: the kind of screen, how it was opened, the wait, the account and the person's key; the IP address sent with it was not stored. Sentry keeps spans 30 days on this plan (its headline says 90; what it keeps spans for is 30). Read on 2026-11-19.
  • The call is written down in the record page-open-timings-go-to-sentry-named-by-the-person-built-from-an-allowed-list, which amends the error record's "no tracing".
